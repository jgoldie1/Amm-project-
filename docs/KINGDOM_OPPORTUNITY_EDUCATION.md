## Aniyah Financial Literacy — Learning Mode for Everyone

The financial-literacy simulator is available to children, teens, college students, adults, creators, families and entrepreneurs. It uses SIMULATED MONEY ONLY unless a separately approved real-money rail is explicitly activated.

### Simulation worlds
- household budget
- first job/paycheck and taxes
- checking/savings
- emergency fund
- credit and interest
- college/trade-school planning
- renting and homeownership fundamentals
- entrepreneurship and business cash flow
- creator royalties/splits
- investing fundamentals and risk
- scam/fraud recognition
- giving/stewardship
- community circulation

### 12-Circulation Challenge
Teach local/community economic circulation as a voluntary educational challenge: attempt to create twelve value exchanges among participating local/community businesses, workers or creators before money leaves that participating network. Never falsely claim that a specific dollar can be technically forced to circulate twelve times. Track network purchasing and supplier relationships instead.

Metrics: local supplier purchases, participating-business revenue, jobs/gigs created, repeat transactions, procurement diversity and estimated local economic retention.

## Emergency Path
A prominent `I NEED HELP NOW` pathway is available from Home, Kingdom OS and Launchpad.

Needs routing:
- food
- immediate safe place
- housing instability
- escape from violence
- medical/emergency direction
- mental-health/crisis direction
- recovery resources
- legal aid
- reentry
- transportation
- child/family needs
- employment
- education
- faith/community support
- I do not know

The software performs resource navigation and triage, not professional diagnosis. Imminent danger routes users toward appropriate emergency/public services and qualified organizations. Minimize sensitive-data collection and never expose a person's help request publicly.

## Launchpad — School Year / College Push

### Pre-K–12
Age-appropriate literacy, numeracy, science, technology, arts, financial literacy, character/service, digital safety and career exploration. Parent/guardian and child-safety controls apply where required.

### Middle/high school
GED awareness where relevant, college/trade exploration, financial literacy, entrepreneurship, coding/AI, media/music, sports/game development, internships, mentorship and violence-prevention/peace programs.

### GED + trades
GED preparation plus pathways into construction, logistics, manufacturing, healthcare-support careers, technology, media, culinary, automotive and other trades through qualified training/credential partners where external credentials are required.

### College/HBCU pathway
College search/readiness, FAFSA/scholarship navigation, application planning, HBCU discovery, study skills, internships, entrepreneurship and financial planning. TRYAMM certificates must not be represented as accredited degrees unless appropriate authorization/accreditation exists.

### Foster-care transition
ID/document checklist, housing-resource navigation, school/college/trade pathway, employment, mentor, transportation, financial literacy, healthcare navigation, legal-aid referrals, emergency resources and stable-adulthood milestones.

## Supplier Exchange
Business capability profile → verification → opportunity match → quote/bid → agreement → milestone → delivery → invoice → payment → verified performance.

Encourage participating businesses to source from one another and expose voluntary discovery lanes for underserved/Black-owned businesses where lawful. Do not misrepresent government certification status.

## Lost Sheep / Find Your Way
One-question entry: `What do you need right now?` Route to work, food, housing, safety, recovery, someone to talk with, education, reentry, legal resources, transportation, faith/community, business creation, creative opportunity or guided discovery.

## Youth peace and violence prevention
Replace attention-only engagement with sports, games, music, studios, filmmaking, coding, trades, entrepreneurship, mentorship, paid/eligible opportunities and community service. Peace Missions can connect schools/neighborhoods through constructive competitions and projects. Do not algorithmically label a child as a future criminal or gang member.

## Reentry / Second Chance
Education before release where institutions permit → GED/trades/digital/financial skills → release plan → housing-resource navigation → Second Chance employers → mentor → work/business → continuing education → service/mentorship.

## Impact dashboard
Aggregate, privacy-preserving measures can include people trained, course completion, jobs obtained, businesses launched, contracts awarded, mentor matches, reentry placements, emergency-resource connections, scholarships, community projects and participating-network commerce.
