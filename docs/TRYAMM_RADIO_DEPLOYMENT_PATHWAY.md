# TryAMM Adaptive Network — Lawful Deployment Pathway

## Release truth

The TryAMM software may select among available networks and provide My World Chirp, adaptive media, accessibility, and fallback logic. It does not itself authorize radio transmission. Production radio service remains blocked until every applicable authorization and safety gate has documentary evidence.

## Recommended launch sequence

### 1. Launch as an application over approved networks

Use fiber, Wi-Fi, Ethernet, mobile carriers, fixed-wireless providers, satellite providers, and approved cloud/edge networks. This is the fastest legitimate route because TryAMM operates as an application and communications service rather than an unauthorized radio carrier.

Deliverables:
- provider and cloud agreements;
- data-processing and security terms;
- coverage, latency, uptime, and support requirements;
- emergency and outage escalation contacts;
- privacy and lawful-process review.

### 2. Build a controlled private-network pilot

Choose one site such as a studio, campus, warehouse, stadium, or test lab. Start with certified equipment and a lawful band/pathway:
- permitted unlicensed operation;
- CBRS through a Spectrum Access System;
- service from a licensed carrier or spectrum partner;
- an FCC experimental authorization for research or market trials.

Do not begin transmitting merely because hardware can be purchased.

### 3. Retain qualified specialists

Required roles may include:
- FCC communications counsel;
- frequency coordinator or spectrum engineer;
- professional structural engineer;
- RF safety engineer;
- certified equipment/integration vendor;
- tower/site contractor;
- cybersecurity assessor;
- insurance and risk adviser;
- local zoning/building counsel.

### 4. Define the radio design

The engineering package must document:
- use case and user population;
- frequencies and bandwidth;
- transmitter power and EIRP;
- emission designators;
- antenna type, height, azimuth, downtilt, and polarization;
- sites, coordinates, indoor/outdoor status, and coverage contours;
- backhaul design and redundancy;
- device types and FCC IDs;
- interference analysis;
- RF exposure analysis;
- shutdown and rollback procedure.

### 5. Select the authorization path

#### Provider or licensed-spectrum partner
Use a carrier/private-network provider or contract with an FCC licensee. Document the license authority and permitted service.

#### Unlicensed operation
Use only FCC-authorized equipment within the applicable operating limits. Accept that unlicensed operation generally receives no protection from interference and must not cause harmful interference.

#### CBRS private LTE/5G
Use certified CBSDs, an approved Spectrum Access System, and the appropriate GAA/PAL rights. Use a Certified Professional Installer where required.

#### Experimental radio
For research, product-development trials, or market trials, obtain the applicable FCC experimental license or Special Temporary Authorization before transmitting under experimental authority.

#### High-band point-to-point backhaul
For licensed/registered microwave or millimeter-wave links, complete frequency coordination, path study, licensing/registration, antenna alignment, and rain-fade analysis.

#### Above 100 GHz / terahertz
Treat as controlled research. Do not market it as a generally available 6G or consumer service until there is an applicable allocation, authorization, certified equipment, and demonstrated safety/performance.

### 6. Equipment authorization

Every transmitter, radio module, antenna combination, and host integration must follow the applicable FCC equipment-authorization rules. Maintain:
- FCC ID and grant;
- test reports;
- approved antennas and power limits;
- integration instructions;
- labeling and user notices;
- RF exposure evidence;
- change-control records.

Do not market or import unauthorized RF equipment.

### 7. Site and tower approval

Before installation:
- obtain owner permission and lease/easement rights;
- complete structural loading and wind/ice analysis;
- obtain zoning, building, electrical, and fire approvals;
- determine whether FCC antenna-structure registration or aviation review applies;
- install grounding, lightning protection, backup power, and secure enclosures;
- document climb safety and maintenance access.

A tower-on-tower concept must be approved by the structure owner and a licensed structural engineer. It must never be assumed safe from drawings alone.

### 8. Cybersecurity and privacy

Required controls:
- device identity and certificate lifecycle;
- encrypted signaling and media;
- secure key storage and rotation;
- anti-replay protection;
- role-based group administration;
- emergency override controls;
- audit logging;
- patch and vulnerability management;
- vendor and supply-chain review;
- location-sharing consent and retention limits;
- incident response and breach notification plan.

### 9. Field acceptance test

Run and retain signed evidence for:
- coverage/dead zones;
- throughput, latency, jitter, and packet loss;
- handover between bands/providers;
- My World Chirp setup time and floor control;
- emergency priority and fallback;
- captions, text-to-speech, one-hand, switch, and screen-reader access;
- interference monitoring;
- RF exposure measurements;
- power failure and recovery;
- cybersecurity penetration/replay tests;
- shutdown procedure.

### 10. Limited pilot

Begin with named users, one or a few controlled sites, fixed hours, a support desk, and an immediate stop authority. Record incidents, complaints, interference, service quality, and accessibility results.

### 11. Production authorization decision

Production may be enabled only when the release committee has:
- provider/license evidence;
- equipment authorization evidence;
- spectrum/frequency plan approval;
- site/structural approvals;
- RF exposure report;
- electrical and safety acceptance;
- security assessment and remediation;
- interference and shutdown plan;
- successful field-test report;
- insurance approval;
- legal/regulatory signoff.

## Honest performance claims

- 10 Gbps is a possible controlled-link or backhaul target, not a universal phone speed.
- 6G is a future-compatibility direction, not a current TryAMM-operated network.
- Terahertz is research-only until authorized, certified, safe, and field-proven.
- Low band improves reach but cannot provide terahertz-class capacity.
- High frequencies improve potential capacity but have shorter range and tougher obstruction/weather constraints.

## Production stop conditions

Immediately suspend the affected radio service for:
- harmful interference;
- authorization expiry or mismatch;
- unauthorized equipment or configuration;
- RF exposure exceedance;
- structural/electrical safety issue;
- compromised credentials or keys;
- failed emergency communications;
- regulator, provider, site-owner, or safety-officer direction.

## External work that cannot be replaced by repository code

TryAMM still requires actual contracts, applications, licenses, certified hardware, engineering stamps, tests, insurance, and regulator/provider decisions. The repository supplies the control system, evidence model, and release gates; it does not create legal authority by itself.
