# TRYAMM low-budget launch and scaling gates

Status: proposed operating plan, not enabled billing, spending limits, sales staffing or live cybersecurity. October 20, 2026.

## Product and delivery
- Publish and browser-test the existing catalog before recruiting a sales team.
- Start with one industry, one deliverable package and five pilot customers. Preserve previously promised free websites; do not count them as paid customers.
- Prove the complete path: offer, written scope, payment, delivery, acceptance, support, renewal and cancellation/refund.
- Keep existing catalog prices unchanged. The $59 average subscription below is hypothetical, not a new price or activated plan.
- Limit revisions, support hours, AI usage and included integrations in each offer. Meter usage before selling subscriptions; obtain approval for paid extras.
- Verify domain ownership, backups and restore procedure, tenant separation, access roles, monitoring and billing webhooks before wider release.

## Illustrative unit economics in USD
Average monthly collected subscription: 59. Variable delivery/support/processing cost allowance: 20. Contribution per retained customer: 39.
Fixed monthly operating-cost assumption: 800. Break-even: ceiling(800/39) = 21 retained paying customers.
At 25 customers: revenue 1475; variable costs 500; fixed costs 800; remainder 175.
At 50 customers: revenue 2950; variable costs 1000; fixed costs 800; remainder 1150.
These are scenarios, not forecasts. They exclude taxes, owner pay, development and any other costs not included in the allowances. Replace assumptions with actual bills and time records; include all fixed costs before claiming company-wide break-even.
If a recurring agent commission is added, subtract it from contribution before recalculating break-even. Track refunds, payment failures and cancellations. Setup fees are one-time revenue, not recurring income.

## Spending and cash controls to implement
- Owner approves an affordable pilot budget before hiring; no expenditure is authorized by this document.
- Configure provider budget alerts and actual application usage caps where available. Alerts alone do not stop charges.
- Keep refund obligations, taxes, commissions and delivery reserves separate from spendable cash.
- Track collected cash, acquisition cost, fulfillment hours, variable cost, support load, refunds and renewals weekly.
- Do not add a salesperson until retained-customer contribution can fund the added cost and delivery capacity is available.
- Target acquisition-cost payback within three months for the pilot; at $39 contribution this means at most $117 acquisition cost per paying customer. This is a proposed management limit, not a market benchmark. Include base pay, commissions, tools and lead costs without double-counting them in the total profit calculation.
- Review retention after 30/60/90 days. Expand only after two consecutive months of positive cash contribution after all actual operating costs, manageable support load and money reserved for obligations. This is an internal decision rule, not a guarantee.

## Sales and workforce
Start with referrals and one qualified part-time salesperson after the delivery path works. Use a written compensation agreement, locally compliant pay and no worker recruitment fees. Record lead owner, order, collected payment, refund and earned commission. Limit access to customer data; use individual accounts and MFA. Review outreach and privacy requirements for the worker and customer jurisdictions before contact campaigns. No outreach or hiring has been initiated.
