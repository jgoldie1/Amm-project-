# TRYAMM Community Peace & Safety Corps

Status: LOCKED PRODUCT / BUSINESS SPECIFICATION

## Mission
Create a visible, trained, de-escalation-first community safety and support network that reduces harm, connects people to help, creates jobs and strengthens neighborhoods without pretending to be police or an unlicensed private-security company.

## Core rule
PEACE FIRST → OBSERVE → DE-ESCALATE → ASSIST → CONNECT → DOCUMENT → ESCALATE TO APPROPRIATE AUTHORITY WHEN NECESSARY.

No vigilantism. No pursuit. No punishment. No detention/arrest powers beyond ordinary law. No weapons program. No impersonating police/security personnel. Licensing rules apply where services cross into regulated private security.

## Roles
- Community Ambassador
- Transit/Business Corridor Ambassador
- Safe-Walk Companion
- Event Peace Steward
- Youth Mentor
- Crisis/Resource Navigator
- Outreach Worker
- Accessibility Support Ambassador
- Reentry/Second-Chance Ambassador
- Supervisor / Dispatch Coordinator

## Services
- visible non-enforcement presence
- directions and visitor assistance
- safe-walk accompaniment
- de-escalation and conflict interruption where trained
- business/corridor welfare checks
- youth outreach and mentorship
- connection to Emergency Path resources
- overdose/first-aid response only within valid training scope
- missing-vulnerable-person escalation pathways
- accessibility assistance
- incident observation and factual reporting
- event line/crowd assistance where lawful
- transit/community wayfinding
- neighborhood cleanliness/hazard reporting

## Not included unless separately licensed/approved
- armed security
- guarding property under regulated private-security contracts
- searches, detention, arrest, interrogation or evidence seizure
- emergency medical diagnosis/treatment outside credentialed scope
- traffic enforcement
- covert surveillance
- facial recognition watchlists

## JARVIS support
Ambassador JARVIS can provide:
- shift assignment and check-in/out
- route and zone information
- de-escalation scripts/training prompts
- Emergency Path resource lookup
- translation/captioning/accessibility support
- incident form assistance
- supervisor escalation
- welfare-check timers
- training reminders

JARVIS never instructs users to confront a weapon, pursue a suspect, or substitute for emergency responders.

## Incident workflow
REPORT/OBSERVE → IMMEDIATE DANGER? → CALL/ROUTE TO EMERGENCY AUTHORITY → MAINTAIN SAFE DISTANCE → ASSIST VICTIM/WITNESS WITHIN TRAINING → FACTUAL INCIDENT RECORD → SUPERVISOR REVIEW → RESOURCE FOLLOW-UP.

Reports distinguish observed fact from user allegation. Sensitive victim data is minimized and access-controlled.

## Training Passport
Required modules can include:
- de-escalation
- trauma-informed communication
- bystander intervention
- conflict mediation fundamentals
- CPR/AED/first aid through qualified providers
- overdose-response education where lawful
- youth safeguarding
- disability awareness/accessibility
- mental-health crisis recognition and referral
- domestic-violence referral boundaries
- human-trafficking awareness/referral
- legal boundaries / no-police-impersonation
- documentation/privacy
- radio/dispatch procedures

External certifications remain external.

## Youth / Lost Sheep pathway
Youth can enter through paid/eligible internships, mentorship, sports/music/coding/service projects and peace missions rather than enforcement roles. No minor is deployed into dangerous incidents.

## Business model
Potential revenue lanes:
- business-improvement-district / corridor ambassador contracts
- malls, campuses, venues and festivals for non-security guest/peace ambassador services where lawful
- transit/community wayfinding contracts
- property-management community ambassador programs
- city/nonprofit violence-prevention and outreach contracts
- hospital/community resource-navigation partnerships
- training programs and employer-sponsored cohorts
- grants/sponsorships for youth peace and violence-prevention missions
- software subscriptions for Dispatch/JARVIS/incident/resource-management tools

If a customer requires regulated guarding/security work, TRYAMM routes to a properly licensed partner or licensed entity rather than mislabeling ambassadors.

## Unit economics
Each contract tracks:
REVENUE → wages/payroll burden → insurance → training → uniforms/equipment → dispatch/software → supervision → travel → admin → reserve → contribution margin.

Target contribution margin must be positive before scaling. Do not use unpaid volunteers as a substitute for paid labor in commercial contracts.

## Impact metrics
- safe-walks completed
- people connected to services
- youth engaged/completing pathways
- incidents de-escalated without injury (carefully defined)
- hazards reported/resolved
- businesses/residents served
- response/escalation times
- training completion
- staff retention
- complaints/appeals
- verified referrals to qualified services

Do not claim crime reduction causality without proper comparative evidence.

## Community Gap Finder integration
Aggregate reports can identify gaps in lighting, transportation, youth programming, food/housing services, accessibility, conflict hotspots or late-night safe passage. Individual victims/reporters are not exposed publicly.

## Community Spotlight
Spotlight verified service outcomes, mentors, youth achievements, businesses and neighborhood improvements—never sensationalize victims or incidents.

## Profitability pathway
The model can be profitable if contract pricing covers labor, insurance, training, supervision, technology and reserves. The most scalable margin comes from combining field contracts with TRYAMM software/JARVIS subscriptions, training, and multi-site operations rather than underpaying ambassadors.

## Launch gates
Before field deployment:
1. legal classification review in each jurisdiction;
2. insurance/workers-comp plan;
3. background/safeguarding policy appropriate to role;
4. written scope/no-enforcement rules;
5. training standard;
6. dispatch/supervision coverage;
7. incident/privacy policy;
8. emergency escalation protocol;
9. uniforms/branding that cannot be confused with police;
10. pilot with measurable outcomes and complaint process.
