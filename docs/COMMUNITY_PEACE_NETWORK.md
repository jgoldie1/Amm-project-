# TRYAMM Community Peace Network

Status: LOCKED PRODUCT / BUSINESS SPECIFICATION

## Mission
Create a healthier community-safety pathway centered on prevention, visible community presence, de-escalation, safe-walk support, youth engagement, resource navigation, documentation, and rapid handoff to qualified responders. This is not a vigilante group and does not replace police, EMS, licensed security, social workers, attorneys, or healthcare professionals.

## Core model
NOTICE → DE-ESCALATE → SUPPORT → ESCORT/CONNECT → DOCUMENT → HAND OFF → FOLLOW UP → PREVENT REPEAT.

## Roles
- Peace Ambassador
- Youth Mentor
- Safe-Walk Volunteer/Worker
- Community Navigator
- Event Safety Steward
- Outreach Worker
- Violence-Prevention Partner
- Dispatcher/Coordinator
- Licensed Security Partner (only where separately licensed/contracted)
- Qualified Clinical/Legal/Social-Service Partner where applicable

## What Peace Ambassadors do
- maintain visible, non-threatening presence in participating zones
- provide safe-walk accompaniment when appropriate
- de-escalate verbal conflict using trained communication techniques
- help seniors, disabled people, youth and vulnerable residents navigate safely
- connect people to Emergency Path, Lost Sheep, housing, food, work, education, legal, healthcare and recovery resources
- report hazards and non-emergency community problems through approved channels
- support schools, events, transit-adjacent areas and business corridors through lawful agreements
- document service outcomes without public shaming or mass surveillance

## What they do NOT do
- carry out vigilante punishment
- impersonate police
- conduct coercive searches
- detain or interrogate people beyond what ordinary law permits
- chase suspects
- publish unverified accusations
- use weapons as part of the TRYAMM volunteer model
- access private user data without authorization

## Youth prevention pathway
SCHOOL / STREET OUTREACH → MENTOR → SPORTS / MUSIC / GAMING / CODING / TRADES / CREATOR PROJECT → LEARNING PASSPORT → PAID/ELIGIBLE OPPORTUNITY → BUSINESS / JOB / COLLEGE / TRADE → GIVE BACK.

This connects directly to TRYAMM Peace Missions, Student JARVIS, Launchpad, Supplier Exchange, Holo Marketplace, Creator Studio, Soul Ascension and Community Spotlight.

Location sharing is permissioned, minimized and time-limited.

## Training baseline
Peace Ambassadors should complete role-appropriate training in de-escalation, communication, first aid/CPR where appropriate, disability awareness, youth safeguarding, trauma-informed interaction, anti-harassment, bystander intervention, privacy, documentation, local law/policy, and when to disengage/call qualified responders.

## Paid business model
TRYAMM can operate a for-profit technology/coordination layer and contract with qualified community organizations/providers. Revenue can come from:
- municipal/community-service contracts
- business improvement district / commercial-corridor contracts
- school/campus safe-passage support contracts where lawful
- event stewarding / community ambassador contracts
- property/community association service agreements
- employer late-shift safe-walk programs
- sponsor-funded youth/peace missions
- institutional subscriptions for dispatch, scheduling, training records, impact reporting and Community Gap Finder analytics
- grant-funded programming through eligible entities/partners

Do not charge a person an exploitative emergency fee for immediate safety help. Consumer safe-walk memberships may exist only as optional convenience services with clear boundaries.

## Pricing framework
Illustrative platform/service pricing, subject to local labor/licensing/insurance economics:
- Community organization software: $99–$499/month
- Small business corridor dashboard: $499–$1,500/month
- Institution/campus deployment: $1,500–$7,500/month
- City/BID/enterprise deployment: custom contract
- Training cohort / implementation: separately priced
- Managed ambassador staffing: priced from actual wages, payroll burden, insurance, supervision and margin; never promise a flat national rate before local economics are known.

## Profitability guardrail
For each contract calculate:
REVENUE − DIRECT LABOR − PAYROLL BURDEN − INSURANCE − TRAINING − SUPERVISION − TECHNOLOGY − TRAVEL/EQUIPMENT − SUPPORT = CONTRIBUTION MARGIN.

Platform Sustainability Engine receives only eligible TRYAMM platform/service revenue. Restricted grants, pass-through wages, taxes and partner obligations are not treated as free cash.

## 3.00× Sustainability connection
Community Peace Network contributes to the broader goal only through legitimate platform/service margins. Target:
`eligible platform revenue / measured infrastructure cost >= 3.00`.

The safety network should not be forced to hit 3.00× on labor-heavy contracts if that would underpay workers or reduce safety. Instead, software/automation/shared infrastructure can raise blended platform margin while service contracts remain responsibly staffed.

## Community Peace Digital Twin
Holographic map layers can show only appropriately aggregated/authorized signals:
- staffed safe zones
- open community resources
- event/school routes
- service demand trends
- unresolved hazards
- mentor/program capacity
- outreach outcomes

Never display a public "crime score" for an individual or predict that a child/person will become criminal.

## JARVIS integration
Personal JARVIS: requests safe walk/resources.
Student JARVIS: mentors/peace programs.
Business JARVIS: corridor concerns and employee safe-walk support.
Community JARVIS: staffing, missions, resource gaps and impact.
Security JARVIS: abuse/fraud/privacy monitoring.

## Business opportunity
This can be profitable if TRYAMM sells technology, coordination, training, analytics and managed programs to institutions while preserving a free/low-friction public help pathway. The moat is the connection to education, work, business creation, accessibility, opportunity matching and measurable prevention outcomes—not confrontation.

## Evidence model
Measure safe-walk completions, resource connections, youth mentor matches, program attendance, hazards resolved, repeat service demand, training completion, partner response time and participant-reported safety. Do not claim crime reduction causation without a valid evaluation design.
