# OmniResilience Pandemic Crisis Level

Status: EXPERIMENTAL / DRAFT FEATURE
World: StreetVerse Chicago + OmniResilience
Release dependency: NO

## Purpose

Build a high-tension, premium pandemic/crisis campaign without turning real public-health history into unsupported conspiracy fiction.

The gameplay target is "survival under system pressure":
- hospitals and emergency services under load;
- food and medicine supply chains;
- businesses closing, adapting or going remote;
- transit changing;
- schools and workplaces moving online;
- testing / laboratory logistics;
- public-information uncertainty;
- community aid;
- vaccine/treatment manufacturing and distribution;
- economic and social recovery.

The intensity can be comparable to a premium survival game while the mechanics remain StreetVerse-specific: city systems, people, logistics, creator tools, business, transit and decision consequences.

## Two clearly separated modes

### Historical COVID-19 Scenario

Uses dated, sourced historical facts and public records.

Rules:
- real people appear only through sourced documentary cards, archival-style timeline entries, public-role summaries or licensed material;
- do not invent secret meetings, crimes, motives, dialogue or medical claims;
- historical information must include date/source metadata;
- disputed claims are labeled as disputed and attributed rather than presented as fact;
- gameplay choices may branch the player's fictional local response, but cannot rewrite real history as fact.

### Fictional Future Outbreak

Use a fictional pathogen, fictional agencies and fictional officials for dramatic storytelling.

This mode can include:
- mystery;
- institutional conflict;
- sabotage;
- bad actors;
- competing scientific hypotheses;
- emergency powers;
- supply-chain corruption;
- black-market gameplay;
- high-stakes rescue and investigation.

Because the people and outbreak are fictional, the game can be much more dramatic without falsely accusing real people.

## Real public figures

Anthony Fauci and Bill Gates may be referenced in the Historical COVID-19 Scenario only through fact-sourced roles and public activities.

Preferred implementation:
- timeline cards;
- news/archive-style screens;
- optional museum/documentary missions;
- sourced public statements;
- player-facing "compare what was known at the time" panels.

For interactive dramatic characters, create fictional equivalents rather than placing unsupported actions or dialogue on real people.

## Five-stage OmniResilience state machine

### 1. NORMAL

The city operates normally.
Player sees baseline:
- hospital capacity;
- transit;
- stores;
- schools;
- offices;
- deliveries;
- resident schedules.

### 2. WATCH

Signals begin changing:
- unusual hospital demand;
- public-health alerts;
- supply price movement;
- PPE/testing shortages;
- rumors and contradictory information.

Gameplay:
- investigate credible sources;
- prepare inventory;
- communicate without panic;
- run early logistics missions.

### 3. LOCAL EMERGENCY

City systems react:
- hospital surge;
- staffing shortages;
- event cancellations;
- school/work changes;
- transit demand shifts;
- delivery growth;
- business stress.

Gameplay:
- essential-worker routes;
- clinic/lab logistics;
- groceries/medicine;
- remote-work setup;
- small-business adaptation;
- community aid.

### 4. REMOTE / QUARANTINE

The city becomes physically quieter but digitally busier.

StreetVerse systems:
- remote commerce;
- Holo Delivery;
- virtual classrooms;
- Middleverse remote work;
- telehealth navigation;
- LIVE creator events;
- remote worship/community gatherings;
- emergency communication;
- virtual business operations.

The player should feel the same Chicago world operating differently rather than entering a disconnected minigame.

### 5. RECOVERY

Recovery is a long gameplay phase, not a victory screen.

Systems:
- reopening decisions;
- jobs returning/changing;
- business recovery;
- medical backlog;
- transit normalization;
- school reopening;
- mental/social recovery missions;
- supply-chain normalization;
- memorial/community events;
- preparedness upgrades for the next emergency.

## Five-layer Chicago integration

### Elevated / L

During crisis:
- ridership changes;
- station crowd state;
- service-frequency changes;
- essential-worker transit missions;
- public-information displays.

### Street

The strongest visual change:
- storefront state;
- reduced/increased traffic by phase;
- queues and delivery zones;
- clinics/testing points in historical or fictionalized form;
- mutual-aid missions;
- emergency/service activity.

### Lower Road / Service

Becomes logistics gameplay:
- warehouse-to-hospital deliveries;
- grocery/medicine movement;
- service entrances;
- fleet dispatch;
- bottleneck resolution.

### Pedway / Subway

Becomes crowd/indoor-routing gameplay:
- station transfer decisions;
- indoor density;
- accessible routes;
- essential worker travel;
- public-information wayfinding.

### River / Deep Infrastructure

Supports:
- freight/logistics;
- recovery infrastructure;
- fictionalized deep-service story missions;
- resilient-city systems.

## Tension system

Tension should come from:
- time pressure;
- limited capacity;
- uncertain but sourced information;
- competing needs;
- disrupted routes;
- resource allocation;
- staff fatigue simulation;
- supply-chain delays;
- consequences that persist.

Do not depend on monsters or shooting to create pressure.

## Resident simulation

Residents react by phase:
- work from home;
- essential work;
- school remote;
- shopping frequency changes;
- transit changes;
- delivery use;
- social isolation/reconnection;
- business closure/reopening;
- hospital/clinic visits represented abstractly and privacy-safely.

No real user's health status is needed for this simulation.

## Creator loop

Pandemic campaign moment
→ cinematic marker
→ Reel / documentary clip
→ timeline/source overlay
→ creator commentary
→ community discussion
→ optional educational follow-up.

Historical mode must visually distinguish factual source cards from fictional gameplay.

## Business/economy loop

Businesses can adapt:
- dine-in → pickup/delivery;
- storefront → live commerce;
- office → remote work;
- school → virtual classroom;
- venue → virtual event;
- delivery demand changes;
- suppliers reroute.

Real-money functions remain server-authoritative and separate from the simulation.

## Premium quality bar

- strong environmental audio;
- dramatically different crowd/traffic states by phase;
- meaningful interiors;
- cinematic but readable UI;
- persistent consequences;
- resident behavior changes;
- cross-layer missions;
- accessible controls and captions;
- desktop/mobile-safe parity;
- no unsupported medical claims;
- no defamatory real-person storylines;
- clear historical-vs-fictional labeling.

## What makes this uniquely StreetVerse

A conventional pandemic game models infection.

StreetVerse can model the entire city:
PEOPLE + TRANSIT + HOSPITALS + BUSINESSES + SUPPLY CHAINS + DELIVERY + SCHOOL + WORK + MEDIA + CREATORS + COMMUNITY + RECOVERY.

That makes OmniResilience a city-resilience simulation rather than a single-mechanic outbreak game.
