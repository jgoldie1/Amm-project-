# STUBBS MOBILITY 360 — CROSS-DISABILITY COMMERCE ROADMAP
Research date: October 8, 2026. Division of All American Marketplace / TRYAMM. Business plans and pricing hypotheses, not validated inventory or actual store sales.

## Positioning
Accessible, value-conscious, transparent buying support for disabled adults, older adults, caregivers, rehabilitation teams, employers and schools. Compete on product fit + trustworthy videos + service + fair **delivered** prices, not price alone.

## Market hypothesis
- High-velocity discovery: folding reachers and basic daily-living aids (estimated Amazon US top-50 reacher search sales ~110,700 July 2026, ASINsight; unverified third-party tracking).
- Repeat purchase discovery: rinse-free bathing wipes, replacement tips, hygiene supplies (before recurring orders verify labeling, supplier lot tracking and recall process).
- Higher average order value: one-handed kitchen independence bundles. TikTok Shop displays examples at ~$47.99-$64.99 but very limited visible sales, so run a conversion test.
- B2B professional category: bathroom safety, workplace accommodations, accessible communication, tablet stands, adapted classroom tools.
- Regulated/risk categories: AFOs, electrical stimulation, hand robotic devices, powered exoskeletons. Never bypass FDA requirements; require professional fit/appropriate partner service.

## Cross-disability departments (future inventory, not current live listings)
1. Stroke, hemiparesis and one-handed independence: reachers, dressing, kitchen, communication.
2. Wheelchair and spinal cord injury: organizers, accessible mounts, compatible cushions from vetted partners, adaptive home equipment.
3. Arthritis and reduced dexterity: jar openers, ergonomic grips, easy clothing.
4. Parkinson's and tremor support: stable drinkware, weighted utensils where appropriate, anti-slip aids.
5. Blindness/low vision: tactile labels, talking clocks, high-contrast devices; test with blind customers.
6. Deaf/hard-of-hearing: caption-friendly communication hardware, visual alerts, text-based customer service.
7. Speech and communication access: symbol boards, accessible switches and AAC partner catalog.
8. Neurodiversity and cognitive access: predictable visual schedules, low-stimulation accessories, clear instructions.
9. Caregiving and bathing: rinse-free hygiene, toileting aids, safe shower seats, adaptive bedding.
10. Independent travel and employment: laptop/phone mounts, bags, vehicle transfer aids through qualified partners.
11. Children: age-specific products only after applicable child-safety testing, labeling and CPSC/CPSIA review.
12. Rehabilitation technology: professional-partner-first for regulated products.

## Rank categories before actual procurement
- Folding reacher grabbers: demand 5/5, differentiation 2/5, shipping 3/5, risk 2/5. Source bundles and low-force grip variant. Competing against ~$9.99 individual listings means avoid impossible $9.99 price matching unless delivered-cost margin supports it.
- One-handed kitchen bundle: demand unproven 2/5, differentiation 5/5, shipping 3/5, risk 3/5. Test unique bundle and instructional videos.
- Button hooks, socks, no-tie laces: demand 3/5, differentiation 3/5, shipping 5/5, risk 2/5. Attach to bundles, avoid single-item ad spend.
- Bathing wipes: demand 4/5, repeat 5/5, shipping 3/5, risk 2/5. Verify safe claims, ingredients and monthly replenish economics.
- Shower chairs: demand 5/5, differentiation 2/5, shipping 1/5, risk 4/5. Use US-based vetted distributors and strong returns/warranty coverage.
- AFO, FES and robotics: demand to validate, medical risk 5/5. Supplier+regulator validation and clinician oversight before marketing.

## Pricing approval worksheet
Retail price must be based on *actual* supplier quote, freight to buyer, duty/tax, payment fee, packaging, chargebacks, returns, acquisition cost and warranty reserve. Do not show target prices as offers.
Go/no-go guidance for early SKUs: documented sample quality; >1 supplier; disclosed delivery window; no unresolved recalls; suitable user instructions; positive per-order contribution after marketing and returns; and minimum margin threshold approved by founder. Review buyer safety and returns separately from margin.
Never describe a product as FDA-approved or FDA-cleared unless independently verified for the exact manufacturer/model/use. CE approval alone is not US authorization.

## Sourcing requests (Alibaba/1688/approved US distributors)
Request business license, actual factory identity/address, 1-unit dropship availability, SKU variants, English instructions, lot traceability, shipping origin and Incoterms, DDP quote with current US tariff, defect rate, independent lab reports, applicable FDA establishment/listing and 510(k)/exemption, US agent/importer role, warranty/RMA return address, replacement turnaround, product liability policy and proof of rights to images.
Never place direct medical-device imports on autopilot before counsel/compliance review.

## Growth engines (subject to verification)
1. DTC store: clear 48-state/50-state delivery choices and transparent checkout.
2. Bundles: One-Hand Starter, Home Independence, Bath Safety, Caregiver Relief, Low-Vision Essentials.
3. Nonclinical courses: skill videos with disabled instructors, captioning and disclosure; never promise rehabilitation outcomes.
4. B2B: rehab discharge planners, local community organizations, employers, schools, disability-serving agencies; purchasing contracts without inducements for federally reimbursed referrals.
5. Creator affiliate marketplace: diverse disability creators paid for genuine reviews; FTC sponsorship disclosure.
6. Authorized installation/repair services: verified insured local technicians; professional scope only.
7. Equipment rentals: after cleaning, hygiene, maintenance, safety and liability framework.
8. Private-label: only after proven product quality, manufacturer agreements, recall/traceability and regulatory responsibilities.
9. Institutional accessible web builder: accessible shops for disabled entrepreneurs in the All American network.
10. Local demonstration events + LIVE commerce: product comparisons not miracle cures.

## Public competitor/social intelligence
Weekly manual or permitted-data review: Amazon/Google Shopping competitor delivered price; TikTok Shop public item prices and visible sales; Meta Ad Library public ads; YouTube captioned product demos; Reddit stroke/disability forums for unmet needs. Do not scrape private accounts or bypass rate limits/ToS, and treat social engagement as directional, not sales proof.
Record competitor SKU, version, actual delivered price, date, returns/warranty, retailer, availability, citations and test results in a sourced scorecard. Do not fabricate reviews, testimonials or urgency discounts.

## 90-day execution checkpoints
Days 1–14: product source scorecard; test 10 physical samples; cross-disability advisory group; legal entity, state sales tax nexus and insurance review; select 5 nonclinical SKUs.
Days 15–30: FDA/consumer-safety category review, photo/video rights, shipping samples and refund simulations, WCAG review, exact pricing and fulfillment terms.
Days 31–60: enable server-authoritative checkout only for individually approved SKUs; support channels; accessible UGC demos; measure first 25–100 paid orders if attained.
Days 61–90: bundle margin improvements, supplier redundancy, email/service follow-up with consent, 5 B2B procurement conversations, decide whether to scale and whether to add regulated partner items.

## Release acceptance
No SKU becomes sellable unless its approval record includes: supplier, manufacturer, model/variant, landed cost, customer price, stock source, expected delivery, regulatory classification, exact allowed claims, service instructions, warranty, returns, and evidence links.
Minimum digital accessibility: touch and keyboard, screen reader, captions, large text, readable contrast, clear error states, optional voice search, no forced medical-history collection.
All health-device content must adhere to FTC evidence standards and FDA rules. Medicare reimbursement needs CMS DMEPOS accreditation/enrollment and bond where applicable; cash-pay sales do not imply Medicare eligibility.
