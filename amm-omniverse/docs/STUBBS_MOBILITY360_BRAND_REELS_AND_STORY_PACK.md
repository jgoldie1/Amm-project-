## First four product-detail drafts

### Folding Reacher Grabber
Short headline: Reach more without extra bending.
Description: A proposed lightweight folding tool for retrieving small, reachable objects in day-to-day routines. We will confirm actual weight, jaw grip, pickup limitations, folded size and whether it is suitable for one-handed handling when the sample and manufacturer details are verified.
Who might find it useful: people who prefer to reduce bending or reaching; household helpers.
Ask before buying: handle comfort, hand strength, safe pickup weight, folded length, return policy.
Avoid: "Prevents falls," "safe for all disabilities" or "guaranteed strength."

### Button & Zipper Helper
Short headline: Small tool. Easier dressing routine.
Description: A proposed large-grip helper designed to guide small buttons or zipper tabs when hand dexterity makes fastening difficult. Actual fit, handle size, sharp edges, fabric compatibility and durability require a sample test.
Ask before buying: handle diameter, hook finish, zipper type, one-hand usability.
Avoid: promising restoration of hand movement.

### No-Tie Elastic Shoelaces
Short headline: Less tying. More going.
Description: A proposed elastic lacing option intended to make everyday shoe fastening simpler. Check actual fastener type, length, shoes, fit and whether shoes remain securely fitted for the user's activity.
Ask before buying: compatible shoes, closure security, replacement parts, sizing.
Avoid: saying all customers can safely walk in them.

### One-Hand Jar Opener
Short headline: Make kitchen tasks feel simpler.
Description: A proposed jar-opening aid for tasks that are difficult with limited hand strength or one-hand use. Exact lid sizes, anchoring, food-safe materials, cleaning and pinch safety require product confirmation.
Ask before buying: mounting method, countertop compatibility, lid dimensions, sharp edges.
Avoid: "100% safe" or "works for every jar."

## Four original product-demo video scripts
Produce these only with a real authorized sample or clearly marked non-product concept animation. A demo does NOT claim therapeutic outcomes. Obtain any presenter's permission and make caption/subtitle tracks.

### Video 1: Reacher — "When the small things are out of reach" (30 seconds)
0–04s: Show a clearly identified actual reacher and a lightweight item lying on a table or floor. On-screen: "A reaching tool for everyday tasks."
04–10s: Close-up of handle and opening/closing jaws. Voice: "First, check the grip, the jaws, and the weight limit."
10–22s: Presenter uses the reacher to pick up a light household item from a safe seated position. Voice: "Here's one example of a simple pickup task. Take your time and follow the instructions."
22–27s: Show how it folds, if applicable; do not put weight on the device. Voice: "Check the product's safe-use limits before buying."
27–30s: Title card: "Stubbs Mobility 360 • Product demonstration • Specifications pending."
Caption transcript: "Here's a reaching aid for everyday tasks. Check the handle, jaws and weight limit. This is one example of picking up a light item while seated. Always read the product instructions. Stubbs Mobility 360."
Safety: Never demonstrate standing on a stool, picking up heavy or hot items, or balancing on a reacher.

### Video 2: Button and zipper helper — "Getting dressed, your way" (30 seconds)
0–04s: Show actual tool on a plain background; label the exact model.
04–10s: Detail of grip and rounded hook finish. Voice: "Small fasteners can be frustrating. Start by checking this tool's grip and finish."
10–22s: Presenter slowly demonstrates one button and one zipper on clothing laid flat on a table; film close enough to see steps.
22–27s: Show compatibility limitations on-screen. Voice: "Not every zipper or button works with every tool."
27–30s: Title: "Practical tools. Honest demonstrations."
Caption transcript: "A dressing tool may make some small fasteners easier to manage. Here's a close-up example. Fit varies by clothing and the person's needs. Check specifications."
Safety: Avoid pulling hard on skin, forcing clothing on a person or implying hand recovery.

### Video 3: No-tie laces — "Shoes without repeated knots" (25 seconds)
0–05s: Present actual unlaced shoe, elastic laces and fastener.
05–12s: Hands demonstrate the manufacturer's installation method at a stable tabletop.
12–20s: Show closing and loosening; display warning that proper shoe fit and secure fastening matter. Voice: "Before everyday use, confirm secure fit and comfort."
20–25s: Title: "One practical option. Not one-size-fits-all."
Caption transcript: "Elastic laces offer an option for simpler shoe fastening. Installation and fit depend on the product. Check the shoes feel secure before use."
Safety: Do not film unsafe walking tests or assert fall prevention.

### Video 4: One-hand jar opener — "Opening the kitchen" (30 seconds)
0–05s: Actual device beside a jar with unbroken factory seal and soft countertop setup.
05–12s: Close-up of base and anchoring according to instructions. Voice: "First check lid sizes, countertop fit and the manufacturer's directions."
12–23s: A presenter demonstrates an approved jar-opening technique with the device, ensuring no uncontrolled sharp/heat hazards.
23–27s: "Clean and store safely" card; show how to release grip without pinching.
27–30s: Title: "Kitchen tools that deserve a real-world test."
Caption transcript: "This tool is designed to assist with opening certain jars. Check the mounting method, supported lids and safety instructions. Results depend on the product and the task."
Safety: Do not demonstrate glass breakage risk, unsupported mounting or injury-prone gripping.
