LEARN • FREE GUIDES

Start with the task—not a diagnosis

These checklists are intended to help anyone compare everyday products. For higher-risk equipment, ask a qualified professional what is appropriate.

Everyday living

How to compare folding reachers

  1. Check length, folding mechanism, weight and grip comfort.
  2. Ask about the manufacturer's maximum object weight.
  3. Review how the jaws handle small or slippery objects.
  4. Confirm safe-use instructions and returns before ordering.

Never use a reacher as a balance support or to lift something above its approved weight.

One-handed routines

Planning a simpler dressing setup

  1. Place clothing and supplies within easy reach while safely seated.
  2. Compare large grips, button hooks and zipper pull shapes.
  3. Check sharp edges, fastener size and fabric compatibility.
  4. Choose a method that is comfortable and does not require unsafe force.

Individual occupational-therapy recommendations may differ.

Kitchen accessibility

What makes an adaptive jar opener useful?

  1. Check compatible lid sizes and material safety.
  2. Understand whether the opener must be mounted or clamped.
  3. Look for pinch, glass breakage and cleaning warnings.
  4. Compare total cost, return terms and real demonstrations.
Caregiver resources

Before buying a transfer or bathroom aid

  1. Confirm the user's measurements and environment.
  2. Check exact weight rating, assembly and installation requirements.
  3. Seek a trained professional's advice when transfers or fall risk are involved.
  4. Verify warranty, replacement parts and seller support.

Generic product photos cannot verify a safety device's suitability.

Digital access

Make every product video accessible

  1. Include accurate captions and a text transcript.
  2. Show close-ups and explain the steps aloud.
  3. Use high-contrast readable text and avoid flashing edits.
  4. Describe limitations, not just benefits.
Smart shopping

Understand rehabilitation-device claims

  1. Identify exact manufacturer, model and intended use.
  2. Ask for credible evidence supporting any health or recovery claim.
  3. Verify what FDA status actually applies to this specific product.
  4. Discuss suitability with your clinician where appropriate.

FDA registration by itself is not the same as product approval or clearance.
